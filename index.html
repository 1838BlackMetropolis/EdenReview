Section 1

Brand Narrative &
Awareness Strategy

The core story of a Black institution built to preserve dignity, memory, and permanence.

Core positioning

Not simply a burial ground.

North Star

Eden is the full-circle answer to a long history of Black dispossession: a place created so that dignity would not end at death, and where the lives of Black Philadelphia could remain visible, protected, and connected to future generations.

A sacred Black cultural landscape created through collective agency — when segregation, condemnation, and the closure of earlier Black cemeteries threatened both the dignity of the dead and the continuity of Black memory. Its power lies in the extraordinary and the everyday resting together.

1902Founded by Black Philadelphians — for you, by you
93,000Lives entrusted to Eden's care, across 17,000 lots
53 acresBlack-owned, Black-controlled sacred ground
Brand promise & primary brand idea

What Eden protects — and how we say it.

Brand promise

Eden protects more than graves. It protects the dignity, evidence, memory, and continuity of Black life — so that what was pushed out of sight is never pushed out of history.

Primary brand idea

From displacement to dignity.
From burial to memory.
From the ancestors to us.

Strategic objective

Drive awareness of Eden as an essential Black cultural institution and convert that awareness into visitation, partnership, sponsorship, preservation support, and long-term financial sustainability.

How Eden should sound

Reverence, agency, intimacy, historical consequence.

Never morbidity, nostalgia, or generic heritage tourism.

  • Sacred, not solemn-only. Center continuity, dignity, kinship, and ancestral relationship — not fear.
  • Historically grounded. Names, places, dates, and institutional history make the significance undeniable.
  • Black-centered and self-determined. Something Black Philadelphians built — not merely a place they happen to be buried.
  • Accessible, not flattened. Clear for families, students, media — with depth intact.
  • Present-tense. Eden is not a relic. It is an active cemetery, classroom, and living institution.

Elevator pitches · three of ten

Say it in thirty seconds.

Ten pitches in the appendix, each a different doorway. Italics are workshop participants' own words.

The F.U.B.U. pitch

In 1902, a group of Black Philadelphians did something radical: they built their own. For generations, Black people had been denied dignity in death. So Black Philadelphia built Eden. For You. By You. Ninety-three thousand souls on 53 acres of Black-owned, Black-controlled sacred ground. That's ours. It has been from the beginning. It still is.

The reinterment / justice pitch

Lebanon Cemetery. Olive Cemetery. The great Black burial grounds of Philadelphia were forced to close. Forced. Other cemeteries were not forced to move. Only theirs. Even in death, the mistreatment continued. But Eden held them. Eden holds them still. What looks like a cemetery is an act of resistance — 124 years of refusing to let Black history be buried twice.

The time-travel pitch

What if you could sit in the same space as Marian Anderson, Frances Watkins Harper, and William Still? Eden is time travel — the past in the present. Ninety-three thousand stories of ordinary people who did extraordinary things, waiting to be heard. Come sit with your ancestors. History ignored is history repeated. Eden won't let you forget.

Audience messaging

One story, different doorways.

The core narrative stays consistent; the reason to care changes by audience.

Black Philadelphians

Eden belongs inside the living cultural map of Black Philadelphia — a place to find ancestors, gather across generations, and claim a heritage that has repeatedly been displaced.

Ask: Visit. Bring family. Research your people. Become a recurring supporter.

National audiences

A nationally significant landscape where abolition, voting rights, faith, medicine, architecture, and civil rights converge — American history understood through Black lives.

Ask: Visit when in Philadelphia. Put Eden on heritage itineraries.

Funders & sponsors

Both a preservation responsibility and a public-history opportunity: 93,000 records and a rare Black-controlled cultural landscape.

Ask: Fund preservation, digitization, interpretation, programming, access.

Media

What happens when Black communities create permanence in response to dispossession? Jim Crow, cemetery displacement, institution-building, descendant memory, urgent need.

Ask: Cover Eden as a living institution — not only during heritage months.

Workstream 1 · Awareness

Restore Eden to the cultural map.

1 · Website

Make Eden's significance immediately legible

  • Primary narrative hub and conversion tool — understand the story in seconds, know what to do next.
  • Clear pathways: Visit · Find an Ancestor / Map · History · Notables · Tours · Support.
  • Lead with the Beginnings story — Lebanon, Olive, the blocked gate, the first burial.
  • Donation and sponsorship visible throughout — not just one page.
2 · Community partnerships

A place to visit, learn, gather, and commune with memory

  • Recurring heritage days, family-history sessions, veteran remembrance, thematic walking tours.
  • Partner with Black churches, Divine Nine, schools, HBCU alumni, genealogical groups, cultural institutions.
  • "That's ours" as the ownership frame.
  • Community ambassadors who bring their own audiences in.
Success looks like

More people understand why Eden matters; more Black Philadelphians see Eden as their inheritance; more institutions see a credible role; and awareness becomes the financial support that protects the cemetery for the next generation.

Workstream 2 · Sponsorship & financial sustainability

Turn cultural importance into sustainable support.

Because preservation without resources is vulnerability. Sponsorship is a primary brand activation, not an afterthought.

Sponsorship positioning

Don't ask sponsors to "support a cemetery." Ask them to protect one of the most important repositories of Black Philadelphia's cultural memory, preserve 93,000 individual histories, expand public access to a nationally significant landscape, and ensure that Black-controlled sacred space survives.

Who to approach

  • Philadelphia-region corporations committed to Black communities, preservation, education, civic life.
  • Foundations focused on African American history, archives, public humanities, genealogy.
  • Healthcare, architecture, higher-ed, arts, faith, and financial institutions whose own histories intersect with people buried at Eden.
Core sponsorship message

Preserving Eden means preserving evidence: names, families, institutions, stories, monuments, burial records, and the physical ground that connects Black Philadelphia's past to its future.

Workstream 3 · Social media

Build social when Eden can sustain it — not before.

Social media is where Eden's community will gather, where events get announced, and where partnerships form in public. It's also the one channel that punishes neglect.

  • Facebook first. The audience is already there; Events are built in and free.
  • Instagram by cross-posting. One post, two platforms — a setting, not a second job.
  • One volunteer, one job. Post events, share news, answer messages. Narrow scope is the point.
  • Events feed the website. Post once on Facebook; the site reflects it — no website edits.
The gate to open it

Eden has the capacity, a content pipeline (tour photography, archival images, notables), governance (who posts, who approves), and a response structure (who answers within a day).

Until then, Workstreams 1 and 2 do the work — and build the content social will need.

Priority sponsorship platforms

Four types of partners.

Match every prospect to one platform — never send a generic ask.

Preservation Partner

Monument conservation, landscape care, archival protection, and preservation of historic sections.

Digital Memory Partner

Digitization of records, interactive mapping, searchable family-history resources, and interpretive storytelling.

Education Partner

School visits, educator resources, student transportation, tours, and public-history programming.

Community Legacy Partner

Free community days, genealogy events, women's programs, veteran remembrance, and intergenerational gatherings.

Appendix C · Partnership pathway

Test small. Learn fast. Grow what proves itself.

1
Reach out
Open the door
Connect with a partner whose mission already touches Eden — a church, Divine Nine chapter, school, alumni network, genealogical group. Lead with the story, not the ask.
Eden's move
  • One warm introduction
  • Share "Why Eden Matters"
  • Ask for a conversation, not a commitment
2
Start small
One easy thing together
A single, low-lift joint action with a clear date and end — small enough that "yes" is easy, real enough that both sides learn: a tour for their members, a family-history afternoon, a "That's ours" visit.
Eden's move
  • Offer two or three options
  • One event, one date
  • Agree who does what — briefly, in writing
3
Build the relationship
Trust while you work
The joint action is the relationship-building. Co-plan, show up for each other, and notice how it feels — communication, follow-through, shared values, whether their people connect with Eden.
Eden's move
  • Co-plan, don't just host
  • Thank them publicly and personally
  • Hold a short, honest debrief
Decision pointWorth continuing?
Yes — go deeperShared values, follow-through, their people showed up.
Not yet — part warmlyKeep the door open; move to the next partner.
4
Deepen
Sustainable work together

  • Propose a recurring rhythm

Summary of work · the data

From scanned pages to a searchable record.

Eden's physical lists were scanned and transcribed. That work powers the notables directory, the lot map, and everything that comes next. It is all yours.

DocumentWhat it is
Original Database from Scanned Word Documents (.xlsx)The first full transcription — every entry kept exactly as written alongside its source page, so any record can be checked against the scan.
Cleaned Database from Scanned Word Documents (.xlsx)The same records after cleanup — standardized names, sections, lots, and dates.
Cleaned Notables Database for Website (Google Sheet)The notables listing as published: name, dates, category, biography, section and lot. Edit this sheet to update the directory.
Cleaned Full Lot Index with Coordinates (Google Sheet)Every one of Eden's ~17,000 lots with geographic coordinates — the data behind Find a Lot and the notables map.

Technical architecture · the recommendation

Stop treating the website as the place where Eden happens.

A powerful, immersive website that is low cost and low maintenance — and social interaction and events on existing free tools.

The website

Self-contained, Eden owns it

Interactive for visitors; no CMS to log into, nothing to patch, no subscription. Get off Squarespace. The only cost is hosting.

Facebook + Instagram

The living room
